The 2026 Tour de France

  • 21 stages over 23 days.
  • Eight mountain stages, five summit finishes, and a single time trial.
  • Can Tadej Pogačar win a record-equalling fifth title?
  • Can anyone finally stop him — chiefly a Giro-winning Jonas Vingegaard?

Race at a glance

  • A Barcelona Grand Départ opening with a team time trial — the first TTT to open the Tour since 1971.
  • The mountains arrive early: the first summit finish is stage 3, in the Pyrenees.
  • Back-to-back summit finishes at Alpe d'Huez on 24 and 25 July — a Tour first.
  • Just one individual time trial: a short ~26 km test at stage 16, Évian → Thonon-les-Bains.
  • A remixed finale — three ascents of the Butte Montmartre before the Champs-Élysées.

Favored to win

  • Tadej Pogačar (UAE Team Emirates-XRG) starts the overwhelming favourite, chasing a record-equalling fifth Tour — and a third in a row.
  • Dominant through the spring, with a deep team around him in Isaac del Toro, Brandon McNulty and Adam Yates.
  • The rare rider who can out-climb and out-time-trial the field on the same Tour.

Who could challenge

  • Jonas Vingegaard (Visma–Lease a Bike) — fresh off winning the Giro; a three-time Tour runner-up and the only man to beat Pogačar over three weeks.
  • Remco Evenepoel (Red Bull–Bora-Hansgrohe) — a 2024 podium finisher and world-class time-triallist, now co-leading with Florian Lipowitz.
  • The young guns — Isaac del Toro, a "mini-Pogačar," and 19-year-old Paul Seixas, chasing the youngest Tour podium in generations.
