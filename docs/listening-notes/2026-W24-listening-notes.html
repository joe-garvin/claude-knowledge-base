01New Discoveries ×3
New
Beatriz Ferreyra
A Distracted God
Room 40Electroacoustic · Tape · ComputerJune 2026

Ferreyra has been working with tape and computer since the 1960s — first at the GRM under Schaeffer, then across five decades that placed her inside the electroacoustic lineage without her becoming canonical in the way the names around her did. ‘A Distracted God’ assembles three previously unheard compositions for Room 40, which is its own kind of statement: not a vault-clearing or a settlement of accounts, but work held back because it was still being understood. Boomkat’s comparisons land on Daphne Oram, Roland Kayn, François Bayle, Valerio Tricoli — those are the right names for the territory — but the simpler frame is that this is what sustained practice sounds like at 86 when it still has something to say and knows how to say it.

New
Debit
Potpourri
NAAFIGuarachero · Acid House · Dub TechnoJune 2026

Delia Beatriz grew up in Monterrey and built a practice in New York, and the distance between those two cities runs through every track here — not as subject matter, but as structure. NAAFI has spent years operating at exactly this intersection, and Beatriz follows up her 2018 ‘System’ with something looser and more assured: guarachero’s triplet syncopation pressing against dub techno’s spatial logic and acid house’s squelch, none of them dominant, all of them changed by the contact. Mixed by Ziür, 13 tracks, 43 minutes. The first record from her where the thesis is already inside the music rather than being made by it.

New
Robert Aiki Aubrey Lowe
Manifestations in the Shadow of an Uncertain Land
Kou RecordsElectroacoustic · Voice · ModularJune 2026

Lowe has spent years building soundtracks — Candyman, Pooka! — that pushed his vocal and modular practice into cinematic contexts, and this feels like a record that took that cinematic scale and turned it inward. Co-produced by Randall Dunn, it works with xenharmonic tunings that sit at the edge of recognizable pitch — close enough to resonate, distant enough to disorient — and with voice functioning as instrument rather than communication. Boomkat’s comparisons are Rashad Becker’s Traditional Music of Notional Species, Penderecki, Ligeti, and Debit’s ‘The Long Count’: that cluster is unusual but accurate. Also in the Download Now alongside the Ferreyra, which is its own kind of endorsement.

02New Releases from Artists You Know ×1
In Library
Ricardo Villalobos & Mohammad Reza Mortazavi
Swamp (Ricardo Villalobos Variation)
LatencyMinimal Techno · Percussion · PolyrhythmicJune 2026

Villalobos is in the local library across multiple albums, and what Latency has released here is a document of what happens when you give someone whose whole practice is about extending minimal structures into unexpected duration a piece that was already built for it. Mohammad Reza Mortazavi’s original tombak composition was, by Boomkat’s account, ‘practically recalling Ricardo’s style of slinky minimal techno sorcery’ before he touched it — a Persian percussion piece that already inhabited the rhythmic space he occupies. The 24-minute variation doesn’t rework the original so much as unfold what was latent in it. A 12” that functions more as a document than a dancefloor tool, and is better for it.

03Wildcard ×1
Wildcard
Horse Lords
Demand to Be Taken to Heaven Alive!
RVNG Intl.Experimental Rock · Xenharmonic · MicrotonalJune 2026

The wildcard slot is for the record that shares a preoccupation with this week’s main picks — xenharmonic tuning, rhythmic complexity, structural patience — but approaches all of it from a live rock direction rather than a synthesis or electroacoustic one. Horse Lords rework 19th century harp standards using microtonal tunings and interlocking time signatures, which is either willfully perverse or completely logical depending on how you read American vernacular music, and the answer is both. Boomkat’s line is “ecstatic experimental rock record that drives a wedge into the dull US indie canon.” The Quietus named it album of the week. The structural invention here is the same kind of invention that makes the Lowe record compelling; the context is just guitars.

04Also Noted ×5
01Boards of Canada — Inferno (Warp) — Flagged in W22 on release day; RA’s full review is now in: “dark, disquieting record shaped by a violent world,” trading Tomorrow’s Harvest’s pastoral unease for “unadulterated menace.” Worth revisiting with the review as a frame if the initial listen was too close to the hype.
02Milan W. (Stroom) — Boomkat describes the new LP as landing “between Arp, Boards of Canada, and Arca”: daydreamy, hydroponic pastoralism with a heavy 80s lilt. Stroom Tier 2; when they pick up something this specifically described, it holds up.
03X Katedral — Anthology III (X Katedral) — Third volume of slowly evolving timbral music from Stockholm, with contributions from Kali Malone, Jessica Ekomane, Maria W Horn, Stephen O’Malley; the label is simultaneously reissuing Anthologies I and II for its 10th anniversary, making this a good moment to enter the catalogue.
04Space Afrika — Quiet Storm (Dais Records) — Newly announced this week; third album from Joshua Tarelle Reid and Joshua Inyang, with Klein, RXKNephew, Kelly Moran, Glenn Ligon artwork. Boomkat frames it as “ambient soul modernism.” September 25 release — flag for revisit.
05Seefeel — Sol.Hz (Warp) — Covered in W18 on release; The Wire gave them the June cover (issue 508), which is a different kind of endorsement than release-week coverage and worth noting. First album in 15 years, their dub record, still worth going back to.

Ferreyra is the pick of the week — work held back for decades, released now because it was ready, and the patience shows in every track. Lowe and Ferreyra occupy the same zone from different directions: both electroacoustic composers building with precision and duration, both landing in the same Boomkat email. The Debit record is the most immediately playable of the three. Villalobos with Mortazavi is what it sounds like when two practices that were already compatible finally meet and neither has to adapt.
